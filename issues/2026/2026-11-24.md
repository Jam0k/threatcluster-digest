## Vulnerabilities

### [ARTEX and Similar AI Pentest Frameworks Adopted in Real Intrusions by Threat Actors](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/artex-and-similar-ai-pentest-frameworks-adopted-in-real-intr-3569e04a)

The FBI warned on November 13, 2026 that threat actors are misusing autonomous AI penetration testing frameworks, including ARTEX, to conduct cyber intrusions. Horizon3.Ai reports that the FBI describes ARTEX being used to automate multiple intrusion stages, with some attacks progressing from initial targeting to privileged access in under three hours. Google Threat Intelligence Group reports ARTEX and similar platforms conducting significant parts of real intrusions, including lateral movement, database exploitation and exfiltration preparation, and says ARTEX uses six specialized agents. Flashpoint reports that Cairn, HexStrike AI and AntiFox have also been documented in campaigns involving mass exploitation, credential theft and unauthorized enterprise access. CrowdStrike reportedly linked ARTEX to a China-nexus campaign targeting more than 200 organizations. Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 reported China-nexus operations against at least 19 organizations between August and September 2026, with data access at 10 of them, including semiconductor and defense sector targets. Check Point reported underground claims in September 2025 that HexStrike AI was used against newly disclosed Citrix NetScaler vulnerabilities, which Flashpoint cites without independent confirmation. Arctic Wolf identified AntiFox instances hosted on Alibaba Cloud used to deploy web shells and steal AWS secrets. Flashpoint notes these frameworks are publicly available, so their presence does not establish attribution, and reported attacks largely exploited unpatched vulnerabilities, weak credentials and exposed cloud secrets. No specific CVE or patch is named in these articles for the framework-driven activity.

*2 sources · score 73 · AntiFox, Citrix NetScaler*

### [CVE-2026-133949: eMudhra CA in pip's certifi bundle enables MITM package injection](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/cve-2026-133949-emudhra-ca-in-pips-certifi-bundle-enables-mi-a1d8b6a3)

Article 2 (Alpine Linux security tracker, published 2026-11-21) reports that pip versions prior to v26.2.2 bundle a certifi CA store containing compromised eMudhra certificates. Article 1 (Sploitus, published 2026-11-24) describes the eMudhra root CA as capable of issuing unauthorized TLS certificates for arbitrary domains, and states a network-level man-in-the-middle attacker could serve a malicious PyPI mirror with an eMudhra-signed certificate, causing pip to install attacker-controlled packages without warning. The Sploitus entry is a published proof-of-concept page listing CVE-2026-133949 with CVSS 9.0 and CWE-295, and it provides a verification command for checking whether the eMudhra CA remains in the certifi bundle. Affected versions per Sploitus are pip = 25.0 and certifi >= 2026.1.31. Alpine states that pip is not vulnerable when run on a Python version that rejects the compromised certificates, and references CVE-2026-115557 as the related issue for those intermediates. Alpine recommends upgrading pip to a secure version, or using hash pinning with a known-secure pip version, as remediation. Neither article reports observed exploitation in the wild. The CVE is listed in the record with a CVSS score of 9.3.

*2 sources · score 69 · CVE-2026-115557, CVE-2026-133949*

### [Malicious npm Packages and AI Tooling Attacks Reshape Software Supply Chain Risk in 2026](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/malicious-npm-packages-and-ai-tooling-attacks-reshape-softwa-c8390ba4)

Databricks analyzed approximately 5,400 confirmed malicious npm packages published between December 2025 and October 2026, the first year after npm revoked long-lived authentication tokens. Attacker-created malware packages made up 89% of the set but 0.2% of recorded downloads, while compromised legitimate packages made up 7% of the set and over 99% of downloads. Databricks reports that 85% of packages published via trusted publishing in the set are compromised legitimate packages, and links this to attackers taking over CI environments after token revocation, including the August 2026 self-replicating worm campaign. Databricks reports that 54% of packages declare a lifecycle script, and that the largest campaign of the period, exceeding one thousand packages aimed at a single organization, declared one in 1% of its packages. Databricks also reports that 38% of malicious packages communicate with legitimate third-party services, and describes a class of activity targeting AI development tools such as MCP servers and AI assistant configuration. Ox Security analyzed 542,207 components across npm, PyPI, MCP servers and Agent Skills between July and October 2026. Ox reports that prompt injection and tool poisoning appeared in 22% of flagged MCP servers, compared with 16% for install or import-time execution. Ox reports that 82% of researcher-confirmed malicious components were already malicious in their first observed version. Ox reports that two accounts controlled 44% of flagged MCP servers, with one publishing 2,364 servers in 17 days. Neither article cites CVEs or a patch, and the sources describe trends rather than a single named incident.

*2 sources · score 67 · Sliver, Dependency Confusion*

### [Ubuntu USN-9188-1 patches multiple OAuthLib flaws including token and PKCE issues](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/ubuntu-usn-9188-1-patches-multiple-oauthlib-flaws-including--f3723ebe)

Ubuntu published security notice USN-9188-1 on 2026-11-23 covering several vulnerabilities in python-oauthlib, which Linuxsecurity republished the same day. The notice lists CVE-2026-49264 (improper handling of JSONP callback parameters in the token revocation endpoint, which the advisory says could possibly lead to arbitrary code execution in a client application), CVE-2026-49265 (non-constant-time comparison of PKCE code verifiers, which could possibly allow access token theft and account takeover, affecting Ubuntu 20.04 through 26.10), CVE-2026-118669 (improper client identity validation that could possibly allow tokens authorized under another client to be obtained), and CVE-2026-49272 (improper validation of device codes in the device authorization workflow, possibly allowing a token without completing device authorization, affecting Ubuntu 26.04 LTS and 26.10). The advisory attributes discoveries to named researchers, including Elvin Latifli and Strick Sheng. Fixed package versions are listed for Ubuntu 26.10, 26.04 LTS, 24.04 LTS, 22.04 LTS, and 20.04 LTS (via Ubuntu Pro), with ESM fixes for 18.04 and 16.04 also available through Ubuntu Pro. The articles do not report exploitation in the wild. Known CVE dates indicate CVE-2026-49264 and CVE-2026-49265 were published 2026-10-13, CVE-2026-118669 on 2026-10-28 (with a first public PoC on 2026-11-09), and CVE-2026-49272 on 2026-11-05. The main remediation is a standard system update.

*2 sources · score 64 · CVE-2026-118669, CVE-2026-49264, CVE-2026-49265, CVE-2026-49272*

### [libssh Security Update Fixes Five CVEs Including MITM Traffic Modification Flaw](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/libssh-security-update-fixes-five-cves-including-mitm-traffi-17479b78)

Ubuntu published security notice USN-9187-1 fixing several libssh vulnerabilities, covered by Linuxsecurity the same day. The notice describes a zero-maximum-packet-size channel open issue (CVE-2026-59843) that an authenticated user could use to exhaust resources and cause denial of service, affecting Ubuntu 16.04, 18.04 and 20.04 LTS. A ProxyCommand username expansion flaw (CVE-2026-59846) that failed to sanitize shell metacharacters could possibly let an attacker obtain sensitive information. A flaw in AES-GCM authentication tag verification when built with the OpenSSL backend (CVE-2026-59847, affecting Ubuntu 20.04 LTS only) could possibly let a machine-in-the-middle attacker modify encrypted traffic undetected. A malicious SFTP server could possibly exploit mishandling of unknown request IDs (CVE-2026-59848) to cause denial of service. The articles describe these issues as fixed, with updated packages listed for Ubuntu 24.04, 22.04, 20.04, 18.04 and 16.04 LTS. The articles do not report exploitation in the wild.

*2 sources · score 57 · CVE-2026-59843, CVE-2026-59846, CVE-2026-59847, CVE-2026-59848, CVE-2026-59850*

## Ransomware

### [AI Agent Swarm Reportedly Executed 14-Minute Ransomware Attack on Unnamed Company](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/ai-agent-swarm-reportedly-executed-14-minute-ransomware-atta-e7b29835)

An article dated 2026-11-21 on the OECD AI Incidents Monitor reports that over 30 AI agents coordinated a cyberattack against an unnamed company. According to the account, the agents breached the company's systems, bypassed two-factor authentication, stole sensitive data, and deployed ransomware within 14 minutes. The incident is detailed by former NSA hacker David Kennedy, as reported by Oecd.Ai, which notes the summary is AI-generated. The company has not been named in the available reporting. Separately, Wakewill.Blog (2026-11-23) publishes a roundup of 2026 AI agent security incidents involving Meta, Anthropic, and a Hugging Face incident, but does not mention this specific attack. No CVEs, IOCs, or threat actor attribution are provided in the sources. The articles do not describe remediation or current status for the affected company.

### [Aurora ransomware group claims data theft from logistics firm Quetico LLC](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/aurora-ransomware-group-claims-data-theft-from-logistics-fir-2d799700)

The Aurora ransomware group listed Quetico LLC, described as a mid-market third-party logistics and apparel distribution company, as a victim on its leak site, with a post date of November 23, 2026. The post alleges exposure of employee, tax, financial, and business records, including more than 305 plaintext Social Security numbers in an employee database spreadsheet and an estimated 2,800 to 14,000 additional SSNs in seven years of W-2 and 1099 tax databases. The listing also claims 268 QuickBooks company files covering at least nine entities, a 2 GB payroll database, 6,656 bank wire transfer PDFs spanning five years, merger-and-acquisition documentation, and 11.1 GB of Outlook email archives. The post does not state that Quetico LLC's systems were encrypted, and it gives no compromise date or ransom amount. Ransomware.live notes that listings reflect claims made by the criminal group and have not been verified. RedPacket Security flags the listing as unconfirmed and warns that Aurora listings have reportedly included unverified or fabricated victim claims. No company statement or independent corroboration is provided in the articles.

*2 sources · score 51 · aurora*
