## Vulnerabilities

### [CVE-2026-129359: Hardcoded Credential Vulnerability in Linux Foundation Magma](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/cve-2026-129359-hardcoded-credential-vulnerability-in-linux--0ca1159f)

A security vulnerability, CVE-2026-129359, has been identified in Linux Foundation Magma versions up to 1.9.0. The flaw involves a hardcoded inter-service credential that allows any gRPC caller to authenticate as a trusted internal service. This vulnerability can be exploited remotely, potentially allowing unauthorized access to sensitive operational data or service disruption. The vendor has not responded to disclosure attempts. The risk is deemed high due to the ease of exploitation and the potential impact on mobile network management environments. Organizations with internet-accessible deployments are particularly at risk and should assess their exposure and security measures.

### [AI-Driven Vulnerability Discovery Surges Amidst Patch Backlog](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/ai-driven-vulnerability-discovery-surges-amidst-patch-backlo-df05eacc)

In 2026, AI tools like Claude Mythos Preview have dramatically increased the discovery of software vulnerabilities, with over 38,000 identified by Anthropic's Project Glasswing. However, only 217 of these vulnerabilities have been patched, raising concerns about the effectiveness of the open-source community's response. Major software vendors, including Microsoft and Google, have been involved in this initiative, but the gap between discovery and remediation is widening. The surge in vulnerabilities has been attributed to AI's ability to find flaws much faster than traditional methods, with a reported 2,500 high and severity CVEs disclosed in July alone. This pace of vulnerability discovery poses significant risks for organizations that may not be able to keep up with necessary patches.

## Threat actors and malware

### [North Korean Hackers Steal $400 Million Using Deepfakes Amid $1.2 Billion Crypto Seizure](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/north-korean-hackers-steal-400-million-using-deepfakes-amid--5f7c463f)

The U.S. Department of Justice seized $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency linked to North Korea and dismantled the FaceStealer deepfake scheme. Concurrently, North Korean hackers are accused of stealing $400 million in cryptocurrency through sophisticated social engineering tactics, including deepfake video calls. The FBI reported that these hackers targeted individuals in the cryptocurrency sector, using AI-generated impersonations to gain access to digital wallets. Analysts believe that North Korea's cybercrime activities are funding its military programs, including nuclear weapons. The total amount stolen by North Korean hackers in recent years is estimated at $13.5 billion. The DOJ's actions reflect a growing focus on the intersection of cryptocurrency, AI, and state-sponsored cybercrime. Investigations are ongoing to track the stolen assets and identify the perpetrators.

### [Kimsuky Targets Seminar Attendees with Spear-Phishing Malware Attack](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/kimsuky-targets-seminar-attendees-with-spear-phishing-malwar-c8cf0367)

A North Korean-linked hacking group, Kimsuky, has been implicated in a spear-phishing attack targeting attendees of a National Assembly seminar in South Korea. The attackers impersonated a senior secretary from a lawmaker's office, sending emails requesting documents related to seminar honorariums. Initial emails lacked attachments, prompting victims to request the missing files, after which a malicious compressed file was sent. This file contained a shortcut disguised as a legitimate Hangul document, which, when executed, triggered PowerShell to download additional malicious scripts. The attackers utilized an online memo service as a command-and-control channel to maintain persistent access. Genians, a security firm, reported the attack on November 16, 2026, and assessed the confidence in attributing the attack to Kimsuky as moderate due to overlaps with previous activities. The malware identified includes a new remote-access trojan called NodeLinkRAT, capable of stealing files and viewing screens in real time.

*6 sources · score 71 · Kimsuky, NodeLinkRAT*

### [New Malware 'NetamLoader' Targets Government Software Supply Chains](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/new-malware-netamloader-targets-government-software-supply-c-018ce123)

Kaspersky has identified a new malware framework named 'NetamLoader' that breaches customized Windows 10 environments used by government agencies in Asia. The malware exploits legitimate software components, including a third-party patch management tool, to infiltrate systems and erase its traces. Detected in a suspicious compressed file within the Windows update folder, it suggests a possible supply chain attack. The malware can execute commands after a delay, complicating detection efforts. Kaspersky confirmed one compromised system but has not linked the attack to a specific hacking group or confirmed the entry method of the malware. The attack raises significant concerns over software supply chain security for public institutions and businesses.

*2 sources · score 60 · NetamLoader*

## Also worth knowing

### [Emergency Update Released Amid Ongoing DDoS Attack on Tor Network Relays](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/emergency-update-released-amid-ongoing-ddos-attack-on-tor-ne-7b50ca0c)

The Tor Project has issued an emergency security update, version 0.4.9.17, on November 16, 2026, in response to a persistent DDoS attack affecting network relays. This attack follows earlier mitigations introduced on October 28, 2026, which aimed to address a BEGIN_DIR attack that overloaded directory requests. Despite these measures, relay operators reported continued disruptions, including unresponsive relays and high CPU utilization during attacks. The Tor Project has not identified the attackers or their motives but is evaluating additional defenses. Most relay operators are affected, and the situation remains as the network struggles to maintain stability.

*2 sources · score 69*

### [Binance Alerts Users to AI-Driven Impersonation Scams](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/binance-alerts-users-to-ai-driven-impersonation-scams-6b43857d)

On June 22, 2026, Binance issued a security alert regarding a rise in AI-driven impersonation scams targeting its users. Scammers are utilizing advanced AI technologies, including voice cloning and deepfake videos, to impersonate Binance executives and support staff. The exchange emphasized the importance of verifying any unsolicited communications through its official verification channels, Binance Verify. Users are warned that scammers may reach out via messaging apps like WhatsApp and Telegram, attempting to solicit funds or investment opportunities. Binance has not disclosed the number of users affected or the total value of lost funds. The alert reflects a growing concern over the sophistication of these scams as AI technology evolves. Binance reiterated that legitimate staff will never request sensitive information like passwords or private keys. The company has not provided updates on any arrests or enforcement actions related to these incidents.

*3 sources · score 52*
