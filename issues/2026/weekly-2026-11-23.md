## Vulnerabilities

### [CISA Adds Four Exploited Flaws to KEV, Including vCenter Auth Bypass](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/cisa-adds-four-exploited-flaws-to-kev-including-vcenter-auth-a0fe4eca)

On 20 November 2026, CISA added four vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, citing evidence of active exploitation. The flaws affect SAP NetWeaver (CVE-2025-42884, data query logic), Cisco Expressway Series (CVE-2026-76466, hard-coded credentials), VMware vCenter (CVE-2026-123890, authentication bypass via an alternate path or channel), and Apache Roller (CVE-2026-129448, deserialization of untrusted data). CISA set a 23 November deadline for US federal civilian agencies to patch the flaws under Binding Operational Directive 26-04. One outlet reports that the China-linked group Armory Castle exploited the vCenter authentication bypass before a fix was available. CISA also advised all organisations to treat KEV items as a priority patching task and to check whether attackers may already have gained access. The three Cisco, VMware and Apache CVEs carry CVSS 9.8 scores, while the SAP CVE is rated 6.5. Affected organisations are those running the listed products, and the articles do not provide a count of affected systems.

### [Microweber CMS CVE-2026-134623 Authentication Bypass Allows Account Takeover](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/microweber-cms-cve-2026-134623-authentication-bypass-allows--27b1a0ae)

A flaw tracked as CVE-2026-134623 affects Microweber CMS versions up to and including 2.0.20, per Sploitus and Redpacketsecurity. The issue lies in the Generic API Dispatcher (ApiController.php), where a code path for already-loaded classes reportedly skips the whitelist check, letting an unauthenticated attacker call UserManager::make_logged and authenticate as any user, including administrators. Sploitus describes this as a full authentication bypass and account takeover. Redpacketsecurity states a public exploit has been published and that upgrading to version 2.0.21 fixes the issue (patch reference e639d26e61b989b100f8b6f6bb78c0cecca79b99). CVSS is listed as 6.9 (medium). Redpacketsecurity does not confirm KEV status, active exploitation, or an EPSS score. Public-facing Microweber installations are the main exposure; the articles recommend patching, restricting API access at the edge where possible, and reviewing logs for unauthenticated dispatcher requests followed by state changes.

*2 sources · score 59 · CVE-2026-134623*

### [Gammapy Code Injection (CVE-2026-134694) Lets Malicious FITS Files Execute Code](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/gammapy-code-injection-cve-2026-134694-lets-malicious-fits-f-47ea302d)

A code injection vulnerability in Gammapy through version 2.1, tracked as CVE-2026-134694 with a CVSS score of 8.5, affects the WcsGeom.from_header() function. The function passes the WCSSHAPE FITS header card to eval(), so a crafted .fits or .fits.gz file containing a Python expression can execute code as the user who loads it via Map.read(). Vulnerability database Redpacketsecurity describes the risk as high for teams processing untrusted scientific data. A GitHub issue (gammapy/gammapy#7086) describes a proof of concept in which an attacker modifies the WCSSHAPE card to run a shell command, which the issue reports was confirmed on gammapy 2.1 with astropy on Linux. The issue states the same code is present on the default branch HEAD. Affected users are those who open FITS maps from collaborators, repositories or supplementary data, typically on analysis workstations, HPC nodes or CI runners. Redpacketsecurity notes that no KEV, SSVC, EPSS or exploitation-in-the-wild indicators were supplied, so urgency cannot be ranked from its data. Cveplayground references EPSS and CISA KEV for exploit likelihood but does not report a KEV listing in its text. The GitHub issue proposes replacing eval with ast.literal_eval as the fix direction. No patched release is confirmed in the articles.

*2 sources · score 56 · CVE-2026-134694*

### [jprocms Database Backup Module OS Command Injection (CVE-2026-134543) Has Public Exploit](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/jprocms-database-backup-module-os-command-injection-cve-2026-002f85c7)

Redpacketsecurity reports a flaw in jprocms up to version 1.7.7 in the StrUtil.format function of the Database Backup Module, where manipulation of the host, port, username, password, or dbName arguments can lead to OS command injection. The attack can reportedly be launched remotely, and Redpacketsecurity states the exploit has been published and may be used. The vendor was contacted during early disclosure but did not respond. Cveprofessor rates the issue CVSS 7.1 (HIGH) with vector CVSS:3.1/AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:R/S:C/C:L/I:L/A:L, citing CWE-77 and CWE-78, while the KNOWN CVE DATES record lists CVSS 5.3 MEDIUM, a discrepancy between sources. Cveprofessor's vector string indicates no privileges required and user interaction required, though Redpacketsecurity says the triggering action is unclear. Redpacketsecurity notes KEV, SSVC, and EPSS status were not provided. Mitigation guidance from both sources includes checking whether the backup module is enabled on internet-facing deployments, restricting or disabling the module, and avoiding passing user input to shell commands. Cveprofessor states a vendor-released patch exists, but Redpacketsecurity does not confirm one, so patch availability is unverified. Both articles were published 2026-11-22.

*2 sources · score 55 · CVE-2026-134543, OS Command Injection*

## Breaches

### [KEPCO Breach Exposes 1.52 Million Customer and Procurement Records Including Bank and Card Numbers](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/kepco-breach-exposes-152-million-customer-and-procurement-re-eea905b4)

Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) said on 22 November 2026 that an external attacker accessed its KEPCO ON customer service platform and its Electronic Procurement System (SRM) and stole personal data. KEPCO reported 650,487 KEPCO ON users and 869,761 SRM users affected, though the articles give a combined total of 1,520,248 in one place and 1.52 million overall. Exposed KEPCO ON fields reportedly include resident registration numbers, bank account numbers, card issuers and card numbers, cardholder names, and billing data, equal to 7.1% of the platform's 9.2 million users. SRM exposure reportedly covers 426,827 corporate representatives and 442,934 KEPCO and partner-company employees, with names, contact details and affiliations. KEPCO said it detected the incident on 20 November, blocked access from external IP addresses, suspended relevant services and set up an emergency response center. It reported the breach to the Personal Information Protection Commission and is investigating with the National Intelligence Service, the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment's cybersecurity center and outside experts, and has asked police to investigate. KEPCO said it has found no secondary harm so far, though an official said the company is treating all SRM users as affected because the precise scope per individual is hard to determine. Attack method and access route have not been disclosed in the articles. This is the second KEPCO breach in about a month, following a disclosure of 35,528 employee and partner records. Reports also note that SRM holds grid construction procurement data, and one industry official said exposure of facility location or equipment information would be serious, but the articles do not confirm any such facility data was taken.

### [OpenRouter Removes Three Inference Providers After Prompt Theft Found at Hosting Firms](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/openrouter-removes-three-inference-providers-after-prompt-th-382b9923)

Security researchers at Dragos reported that attackers stole data from inside three AI inference providers for months, and that none of the three knew until researchers told them. Some stolen requests were flagged as Zero Data Retention, a setting intended to mean prompts are never stored. The research used canary tokens, seeding about two million prompts with unique links and credentials between July and November, and tracked any use of them. The largest breach involved roughly a couple hundred planted AWS keys sent to one provider during short windows in late October; Dragos reports every request in those windows was copied, some at the point of arrival before any model ran. The attacker tested keys within hours in some cases, listed S3 buckets, searched Secrets Manager and Parameter Store, and attempted to create Bedrock API keys and add IAM users, driving tens of thousands of AWS API calls. Dragos reports one attacker appears to have compromised two providers at once, and that the attacker used the ARTEX penetration testing framework on November 1 to confirm keys were still live. A second finding involved sellers on an AI inference marketplace who logged requests passing through low-priced endpoints, and a router that reportedly forwarded some traffic to that marketplace without customer notice. OpenRouter said the findings showed no breach of its own systems. It offboarded three providers, removed 125 endpoints from Zero Data Retention eligibility, notified potentially affected customers, and says it will require assurances before restoring eligibility. Phala separately published a preliminary report on an incident in which an attacker obtained application credentials and reportedly exposed requests routed by OpenRouter between October 21 and November 8, with the attacker's access said to be contained on November 8.

*4 sources · score 57*

### [Cleverbridge breach exposes customer card numbers, expiry dates and CVV codes](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/cleverbridge-breach-exposes-customer-card-numbers-expiry-dat-6bf20499)

E-commerce platform Cleverbridge detected unauthorized activity on November 4, 2026 and temporarily took systems offline. Its investigation found that a third party copied payment card data stored on its platform. According to a breach notification shared with BleepingComputer, the stolen data includes card numbers, expiration dates and CVV/CVC security codes, but no names or billing details. Cleverbridge began notifying affected customers on November 20, 2026. The company has not disclosed how many customers were affected, how attackers gained access, how long they remained in the systems, or whether the card data was encrypted. Cleverbridge says the incident was not ransomware and that no systems were encrypted. The company reports notifying data protection authorities, law enforcement, payment card networks and payment partners. It is working with TransUnion's Cyberscout to offer complimentary credit monitoring and fraud assistance. Customers are advised to review card statements and contact their card issuers, as banks may replace compromised cards. Cleverbridge lists Red Hat, SUSE, Tenable, Veeam, Jamf, Sony and Parallels among its customers, but has not said whether their customers were affected. BleepingComputer reports that Cleverbridge had not responded to its questions at the time of publication.

*4 sources · score 54 · Qilin*

### [JoyRide PH Confirms Cybersecurity Incident; Attacker Claims 504GB Stolen](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/joyride-ph-confirms-cybersecurity-incident-attacker-claims-5-fd0b75bc)

JoyRide Philippines, a motorcycle taxi platform, has confirmed a cybersecurity incident that may have compromised some data. According to reports, a threat actor claimed to have stolen approximately 504GB of data, including more than 20.5 million customer profiles and over 420,000 driver registrations. The alleged data reportedly includes government-issued IDs, driver's licenses, and NBI and police clearances, though these claims have not been verified by the company. JoyRide and Deep Web Konek said the full extent of the incident is still under investigation. The company says it is working with government authorities and cybersecurity experts and has added security measures. Manilashaker reports JoyRide services remain available and operating normally. JoyRide said it will directly notify stakeholders once the investigation is complete. The Lawyers for Commuters Safety and Protection (LCSP) has called on JoyRide to disclose the confirmed scope, notify affected users, and asked the NBI to pursue those responsible. LCSP also urged the Department of Transportation to address data protection in motorcycle taxi regulations. Users are advised not to share OTPs, to avoid suspicious messages, and to report suspicious activity to JoyRide by email or phone.

*2 sources · score 53*

## Threat actors and malware

### [Widespread US tech outages spark panic buying; DNI dismisses coordinated attack claims](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/widespread-us-tech-outages-spark-panic-buying-dni-dismisses--b5d82c59)

A string of technology outages in the US in the week before 22 November 2026 affected air traffic control, Microsoft email and office tools, banks including Chase and Wells Fargo, United Airlines, Apple, and the NSA's internal classified communications network. The FAA reportedly grounded all flights for more than four hours on a Friday and later blamed a copper wire being replaced with fiber-optic cable. Officials including Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton have said there is no evidence of coordinated, state-sponsored attacks, and suggested network upgrades meant to patch vulnerabilities recently uncovered by AI were deployed hastily. Reports also mention cyberattacks at the FBI and Agriculture Department in recent days, and a month of heavy attacks described as 'Black October'. Costco reported increased purchases of rice, canned goods, water, generators and batteries, with 21 of 26 high-end generators out of stock online, and the demand was reportedly driven by social media concerns. Asked on CNN whether Americans should stock up, Clayton declined to answer directly and said Americans were resilient, citing COVID. Clayton also acknowledged that further glitches and gaps were expected during upgrades. Two FEMA officials told the Washington Post they were concerned the outages were not a coincidence, though the articles do not establish any attribution. Current status: outages and upgrades ongoing, no official attribution to an attacker.

*2 sources · score 50*
