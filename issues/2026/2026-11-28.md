## Breaches

### [Third-party marketing vendor breach exposes JPMorgan Chase customer data; n0n leaks data](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/third-party-marketing-vendor-breach-exposes-jpmorgan-chase-c-af4f3e2c)

JPMorgan Chase learned in mid-November 2026 that third-party marketing vendors were targeted in a cyberattack, and says the attackers accessed certain marketing data that may include some customer names and email addresses. Cpomagazine reports that at least four dozen companies, including Visa and MasterCard, were also affected by the external hacking incident. The extortion group n0n claimed responsibility on 16 November 2026 by listing JPMorgan on a dark web leak site alongside 59 others. Teiss reports that after ransom negotiations failed, n0n published a database it says contains about 16,000 customer email addresses tied to the Chase Sapphire Reserve programme, plus marketing engagement records and phone numbers and account IDs, and 124 bank employee email addresses. Cpomagazine cites independent analyses reporting 16,000 records, including at least 100 JPMorgan employees, though the bank has not disclosed the total number affected. JPMorgan says the breach caused no operational or customer service disruption and that its internal infrastructure was unaffected. The bank is offering complimentary credit monitoring and has notified White House officials, though it says the incident did not meet the SEC reporting threshold. Teiss reports the bank separately attributed a service outage to a software glitch and said it found no evidence of malicious activity linked to it. Cpomagazine notes n0n is a cyber extortion actor that reportedly uses AI to develop vulnerability-probing scripts, and that the FBI recently warned of ARTEX tool use, though no link to this breach is established.

## Threat actors and malware

### [Thai DIPROM customer portal data allegedly leaked on dark web (484,000 records claimed)](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/thai-diprom-customer-portal-data-allegedly-leaked-on-dark-we-dc5715e6)

A threat actor claims to have accessed the customer service portal of Thailand's Department of Industrial Promotion (DIPROM), a department under the Ministry of Industry, and to have obtained 484,000 records. The actor's claimed breakdown is more than 304,000 registered users, 179,500 researcher entries and 91 expert consultant profiles. According to the actor, the data includes 13-digit Thai national ID numbers, phone numbers, emails, addresses and expert CVs, plus infrastructure details and alleged cloud storage credentials. The claim was reported by Dark Web Intelligence on X, which labels it as unverified. DIPROM has not publicly confirmed an incident, and the record counts come solely from the actor. The reporting does not describe how access was obtained. Portal users and listed consultants are advised to watch for targeted phishing. A separate weekly dark web newsletter published on LinkedIn on 27 November 2026 covers other incidents and does not report on the DIPROM claim.

*2 sources · score 64*
