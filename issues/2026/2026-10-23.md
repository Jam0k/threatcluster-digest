### [Critical RCE Flaws Discovered in Linux KSMBD with CVSS 9.0 Ratings](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/critical-rce-flaws-discovered-in-linux-ksmbd-with-cvss-90-ra-86e271a1)

Two remote code execution vulnerabilities in the Linux kernel's KSMBD, rated 9.0 by Trend Micro's Zero Day Initiative, were disclosed this week. The first flaw, ZDI-CAN-33774, allows exploitation without network authentication, affecting any server running KSMBD on port 445. The second flaw, ZDI-CAN-33549, requires an RDMA-capable interface and a writable KSMBD. Both vulnerabilities currently lack fixes in the mainline kernel and do not yet have CVE identifiers. The flaws were reported to public mailing lists, and while a patch for ZDI-CAN-33549 has been submitted, it has not been confirmed. Administrators can check for KSMBD activity using specific system commands. The disclosure has prompted critical responses from kernel maintainers regarding the reporting process.

### [High-Severity BIND 9 Vulnerability CVE-2026-94300 Allows Remote Code Execution](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/high-severity-bind-9-vulnerability-cve-2026-94300-allows-rem-7f41ec67)

The Internet Systems Consortium (ISC) released patches for a BIND 9 vulnerability, CVE-2026-94300, on October 21, 2026. This flaw affects all BIND versions from 9.11.0 to 9.21.26 and allows an attacker to execute remote code by replaying GSS-TSIG signed UDP messages. The vulnerability has a CVSS score of 8.1 and is classified as a race condition and double free. Although ISC is not aware of any active exploitation, the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security and Japan Registry Services have issued urgent advisories recommending immediate upgrades. The flaw was discovered during internal testing, with additional credit given to researcher Dave Aitel for reporting it. No workarounds are available, and affected users are urged to upgrade to patched versions 9.20.30 or 9.21.27.

## Threat actors and malware

### [Spyware Campaign Targets iPhones via Legitimate Websites](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/spyware-campaign-targets-iphones-via-legitimate-websites-91e21a79)

Cybercriminals have exploited thousands of legitimate websites to deliver spyware targeting iPhones, enabling crypto theft without user interaction. Researchers identified two campaigns linked to suspected Chinese hackers, affecting at least 6,600 websites with a combined traffic of 49 million monthly visits. One notable incident involved an Instagram ad that infected a financial institution employee's device, stealing login credentials and cryptocurrency. The attackers utilize expired domains and malicious advertising code to redirect users to spyware. Tools used in these attacks include sophisticated exploit kits believed to have leaked from state intelligence agencies. The campaigns reflect a shift towards spyware delivery through routine web browsing rather than traditional phishing methods. Current efforts are focused on older, unpatched iOS versions, with indications that attackers are preparing updates for newer vulnerabilities.

## Also worth knowing

### [AI Models Show High Success in Planning Infrastructure Attacks](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/ai-models-show-high-success-in-planning-infrastructure-attac-dfaebe13)

A study by cybersecurity firm Frenos revealed that leading AI models successfully devised viable cyberattack plans against critical infrastructure, including water and power systems, in approximately 90% of attempts. The research tested 10 AI models across 16,980 scenarios, with three top commercial models achieving success rates between 89.5% and 92.9%. In contrast, the best foreign open-weight model succeeded 36.2%, while the top U.S. model only managed 5.2%. Despite these findings, the models often missed critical operational steps and required human direction for target identification and execution. The study comes amid rising concerns over vulnerabilities in industrial systems, highlighted by recent attacks on a Mexican water utility and a French supermarket's refrigeration systems. Frenos recommended enhanced security measures, including network segmentation and stricter access controls, to mitigate risks from AI-assisted reconnaissance.
