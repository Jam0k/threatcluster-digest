## Vulnerabilities

### [OpenResty stream-lua use-after-free flaw CVE-2026-136412 disclosed with public PoC](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/openresty-stream-lua-use-after-free-flaw-cve-2026-136412-dis-44dec0af)

CVE-2026-136412 is a use-after-free vulnerability in OpenResty's stream-lua-nginx-module, affecting versions before 0.0.20rc4, per Redpacketsecurity. A receiveuntil() pattern iterator can be garbage-collected while still referenced by the socket, so later reads or socket closure may access freed memory. Redpacketsecurity states a remote peer may be able to trigger this by interrupting or closing a cosocket connection, potentially crashing or corrupting the nginx worker. Sploitus lists an exploit for the same CVE, with a lab-validated description stating a remote peer driving a read error could corrupt runtime memory; the Sploitus article includes a PoC driver and a fix referenced as commit 7581c68b. Redpacketsecurity notes that exploitation depends on a specific runtime condition requiring a stream-lua application using receiveuntil on a socket that can be driven into a read error. Redpacketsecurity states the supplied data does not establish whether exploitation is active or widespread. The CVE record lists a CVSS score of 9.2 and was published 2026-11-24.

### [WPIDE CSRF Nonce Bypass Enables Unauthenticated RCE (CVE-2026-124251)](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/wpide-csrf-nonce-bypass-enables-unauthenticated-rce-cve-2026-684593bf)

A CSRF nonce bypass in the WPIDE WordPress plugin (versions 3.6.0 through 3.6.4, per the CVE record) allows unauthenticated remote code execution, tracked as CVE-2026-124251 with a CVSS score of 8.8. The flaw, disclosed on 2026-11-10 by Patchstack as a CNA, is an incomplete fix for CVE-2026-57766 (published 2026-07-02). The security gate validated nonces only when the page parameter exactly matched the main slug, so the seven submenu slugs containing '#/' fragments skipped CSRF validation. An attacker can host a forged auto-submitting form that makes a logged-in administrator's browser save a PHP snippet via WPIDE's Snippets feature, which executes during the save request. Stored snippets can be re-run on demand via req=/snippets/execute, enabling persistence. Cyberscoop reports the researcher verified the bypass against 3.6.0 with live tests and source review confirmed 3.6.2 is affected; the CVE record marks the plugin unaffected from 3.6.5. Cyberscoop's report states it was generated with the help of GLM-5.3-Flash. Aviatrix.Ai describes the flaw as a patch-bypass vector and notes it affects any site running the listed versions, but the articles do not report observed exploitation in the wild.

*2 sources · score 72 · CVE-2026-124251, CVE-2026-57766, CSRF*

## Ransomware

### [Rhysida ransomware group claims 8TB theft from City of El Cajon](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/rhysida-ransomware-group-claims-8tb-theft-from-city-of-el-ca-eba77493)

The City of El Cajon, California confirmed a "network security incident" on Sept. 27, according to City Manager Graham Mitchell, who said a third-party forensic firm was engaged and the investigation is ongoing. The Rhysida ransomware group has claimed responsibility and published a list of records on its dark web page, which NBC 7 San Diego and KPBS report as roughly eight terabytes of city data. Claimed contents include police internal affairs files, undercover unit rosters, names and phone numbers of informants, 911 call recordings with GPS and caller data, and IRS documents for more than 1,000 employees. Other claimed data includes HR, city attorney and council records, and public works and utility billing information for 250,000 customers. Neither Mitchell nor a city spokesperson commented on the claimed data, and the city has said it is fully operational. A University of San Diego cybersecurity professor said it is too soon to determine what data is actually exposed and that the city would need to confirm its accuracy. Ransomware.live said it discovered the breach this month. An NBC 7 source said the data includes employee W-2s and health data, though this is not independently confirmed in KPBS reporting.

## Breaches

### [Ground-Segment Cyber Weaknesses Expose Satellite Services to Disruption](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/ground-segment-cyber-weaknesses-expose-satellite-services-to-65c43dc7)

Recorded Future's Insikt Group warns that ground-based infrastructure supporting satellites is an increasingly consequential attack surface, as 4,517 satellites were launched in 2025 (87% commercially owned) within a space economy reported at $686 billion. The analysis cites the February 2022 AcidRain wiper malware attack, which reportedly disabled tens of thousands of modems on Viasat's KA-SAT network after attackers entered through a misconfigured VPN appliance, affecting Ukrainian military communications and disrupting around 5,800 German wind turbines' remote monitoring. Recorded Future also describes 2025 attacks on Iranian commercial shipping, where the group Lab-Dookhtegan reportedly gained administrator-level access through Fanava Group, an Iranian IT provider, and disabled satellite communications and ship-tracking software on dozens of vessels. A separate incident last August reportedly left an oil tanker without communications for 30 hours, with investigators focusing on satellite terminals and remote-maintenance links as possible entry points. Neither article cites specific CVEs or detection indicators. Both articles frame the risk as growing with the expansion of orbital services, including proposed orbital AI data centers, and emphasise securing the full ground segment, supply chain and terrestrial networks.

*2 sources · score 63 · AcidRain*

## Threat actors and malware

### [Google Warns of Autonomous AI-Driven Cyberattacks Compressing Attack Timelines](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/google-warns-of-autonomous-ai-driven-cyberattacks-compressin-a07df77e)

Google's Threat Intelligence Group report of 16 November documented threat actors using AI to find and exploit software flaws with no human intervention, marking Google's first observation of this behaviour. According to research cited in the report, an AI-assisted campaign compromised eleven organizations in 26 seconds. GreyNoise research described an attack campaign investigators believe used AI to automate significant portions of its operations, and in one incident at a US high school the attacker reportedly gained domain administrator privileges in seven minutes. The automated framework described by Google was reportedly interested in French media, entertainment and education websites. Google states its findings do not establish that every stage of these attacks occurred without human involvement, only that significant portions can now be automated. Google Cloud CISO Chris Betz, in a Help Net Security interview, argues human-reaction-time baselines are no longer viable and expects autonomous defensive agents to change analyst roles. A LinkedIn commentary by David Newey speculates that recent IT outages may be linked to AI-driven attacks but notes a more mundane explanation involving technical debt is possible, and cites a reported 14-minute AI-agent-swarm intrusion relayed by David Kennedy. No specific CVE identifiers, IOCs or patches are provided in these articles.

*3 sources · score 61*

## Also worth knowing

### [Russia's alleged nuclear-armed 'Sputnik-S' satellite: effects of a space nuclear detonation on satellites](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/russias-alleged-nuclear-armed-sputnik-s-satellite-effects-of-64b32b88)

German media cited intelligence assessments that Russia could launch a nuclear-armed satellite, named Sputnik-S, as early as December; Moscow denies the reports and no launch has been confirmed. Reporting uses the 1962 Starfish Prime test as a reference, which damaged satellites including Telstar 1 and the UK's Ariel 1 and caused streetlight outages in Hawaii. A study published November 6, 2026 by researchers from the University of Wisconsin–Madison and Princeton University, which has not undergone peer review, models a hypothetical high-altitude detonation in which about 98% of modelled satellites could be disabled within a week. The authors say electrons released after a blast could form an artificial radiation belt that degrades satellites on repeated passes, and that disabled satellites could increase collision and debris risk. Analysts quoted by RFE/RL say most satellites, including Starlink, are not hardened against nuclear radiation, and an electromagnetic pulse could disrupt ground electrical systems. Reporting does not confirm that a weapon has been developed or deployed.

*2 sources · score 54*
