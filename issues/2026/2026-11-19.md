## Vulnerabilities

### [Global Alert Issued for Critical Java Vulnerability](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/global-alert-issued-for-critical-java-vulnerability-af604572)

On November 18, 2026, multiple governments issued warnings about a critical vulnerability in Java, known as Java4Shell, which could allow hackers to execute malicious code remotely. Discovered by the U.S. technology firm Calif, the flaw affects Java's memory functions and poses a significant risk to systems across various sectors, including finance and government. Authorities from the U.S., South Korea, Germany, and the UK, along with Tencent, urged organizations to apply security patches immediately. The vulnerability could lead to unauthorized access and control over affected servers. As of now, no successful exploitation has been reported, but the potential for attacks remains high. The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has emphasized the urgency of patching this flaw, which has been linked to a recent increase in cybersecurity risks exacerbated by AI advancements.

*3 sources · score 76 · Java4Shell*

### [Critical RCE Vulnerability in Synology DSM Exploited in the Wild](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/critical-rce-vulnerability-in-synology-dsm-exploited-in-the--afc869a6)

A critical vulnerability, CVE-2024-10441, in Synology DiskStation Manager (DSM) and BeeStation OS allows remote attackers to execute arbitrary code. This flaw affects DSM versions prior to 7.2-64570-4, 7.2.1-69057-6, and 7.2.2-72806-1, and BeeStation OS versions before 1.1-65374. The vulnerability is in the wild, posing an extremely high risk to users with internet-accessible NAS devices. Attackers can compromise systems without needing credentials or user interaction, making this a serious threat. Synology has released patches, and users are urged to apply them promptly. Organizations should also restrict management access and review logs for suspicious activity. The risk extends to both households and organizations utilizing these systems.

*2 sources · score 73 · CVE-2024-10441*

### [Critical Cryptographic Failure in Jira Data Center Docker Images](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/critical-cryptographic-failure-in-jira-data-center-docker-im-8a4d93fe)

A critical cryptographic failure vulnerability, CVE-2026-91403, was discovered in non-Atlassian dependencies used in Jira Data Center Docker images. This vulnerability, introduced in versions 10.3.26 and 11.3.12, has a CVSS score of 9.1, allowing unauthenticated attackers to expose sensitive assets without user interaction. Affected systems include Jira Core Data Center and Jira Software Data Center, which Atlassian recommends upgrading to versions 10.3.27 or 11.3.13 or higher. The National Vulnerability Database indicates that this flaw can lead to unauthorized access to critical data. While the vulnerability is assessed as non-critical for Atlassian's application, it still poses significant risks due to its high impact on confidentiality and integrity. Users are urged to apply the recommended updates promptly to mitigate risks.

*2 sources · score 73 · CVE-2026-91403, Cryptographic Failure, XSS*

### [OpenSSH Password Bypass Vulnerability CVE-2026-123158 Exploited in Musl Systems](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/openssh-password-bypass-vulnerability-cve-2026-123158-exploi-5bcadbac)

A vulnerability, CVE-2026-123158, allows password authentication bypass in OpenSSH on musl-based systems, including Alpine Linux, OpenWrt, and Yocto, where `sshd` is built without PAM. The flaw arises from musl's `crypt()` function returning `*` for keys longer than 256 bytes, which is then incorrectly validated against the stored password hash. This enables logins with any password of 257 bytes or longer for accounts with a `*` in their `/etc/shadow` file. The vulnerability was publicly disclosed on November 5, 2026, and was added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 9, indicating. A proof-of-concept (PoC) was released on November 6, demonstrating the exploit. OpenSSH has released a fix in version 10.6p2. Administrators are urged to update their systems immediately to mitigate this risk.

*2 sources · score 73 · CVE-2016-6210, CVE-2026-123158, CVE-2026-99999, Pam, Sshole*

## Breaches

### [Five-Year Cyberespionage Operation Targeting Bulgarian Government Uncovered](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/five-year-cyberespionage-operation-targeting-bulgarian-gover-b0db72cd)

A significant cyberespionage operation was revealed in Bulgaria, where hackers accessed government networks from 2021 until August 2026, stealing at least 130 gigabytes of sensitive information, equivalent to over 62 million pages of documents. The breach was announced on November 18, 2026, by Ivan Vasilev, the Minister of Innovation and Digital Transformation. The attackers infiltrated systems used for state document exchanges, and while no personal data leakage has been reported, the operation is considered espionage rather than financially motivated cybercrime. The breach was detected following the implementation of a new national cybersecurity system. Investigations are ongoing, and the identities of the attackers remain unknown. The government plans to conduct inspections at affected institutions and enhance cybersecurity measures.

## Also worth knowing

### [Gottheimer and Fong Introduce BRIDGE AI Act to Combat AI Cyber Threats](https://threatcluster.io/cluster/gottheimer-and-fong-introduce-bridge-ai-act-to-combat-ai-cyb-73a5e32c)

On November 18, 2026, Congressmen Josh Gottheimer and Vince Fong announced the BRIDGE AI Act, aimed at enhancing the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency's (CISA) capabilities to defend against AI-driven cyberattacks. The legislation responds to a surge in attacks targeting critical infrastructure, including schools, hospitals, and water systems, attributed to adversaries like China, Iran, and Russia. The BRIDGE AI Act proposes a pilot program allowing 75 to 125 private-sector AI specialists to assist CISA in hunting for intruders, fixing vulnerabilities, and testing AI tools for security flaws. The urgency of this initiative is underscored by recent attacks that have disrupted essential services and highlighted CISA's lack of AI expertise. The bill is positioned as a bipartisan effort to bolster national security and protect American families from growing cyber threats.
